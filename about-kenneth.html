About Kenneth

Kenneth Kron: security leader, coach and Aikido teacher

Kenneth Kron is a former CISO and 6th degree Quantum Aikido black belt who advises leaders on security and on the decisions that matter most.

Who I work with

I work in two lanes. The first is coaching for leaders, founders and practitioners who face a consequential decision, or who understand a problem but have not yet found a way through it. The second is security leadership for growing companies: fractional CISO work, enterprise security reviews, and governance for AI. Both begin the same way, with careful listening.

I would rather spend an hour understanding what is at stake than open with a framework and fit your situation to it. Most of the value in my work comes from asking the question that changes what the real problem is, and from helping the people around the table say what they already know.

The people who come to me are usually capable and busy. A security lead may be asked to justify a budget to a board that does not speak the language. A founder may be answering a customer's questionnaire at midnight. A senior practitioner may be sensing that the work no longer matches what they care about. In each case I try to make the problem smaller and clearer, so that the next step becomes obvious.

Security career

I have worked in security for more than twenty years, across services, product security and executive leadership. In 2009 I joined Security Consortium as a senior professional services engineer and became Director of Services, leading a team of three to five analysts and penetration testers. At Code Integrity I performed static analysis of a phone software stack. I spent 2011 to 2015 at Truvantis.

I later completed the Insight Data Science fellowship, where I built one-time-password infrastructure on HashiCorp Vault and Boundary. At Vigilent I was Security Lead from July 2021 and CISO from March 2022, on a contract basis.

What ties these roles together is a habit of asking what the business needs to do next, then working backward to the security that makes it possible. I help companies pass enterprise security reviews, earn customer trust, and give their boards a clear view of risk. I prefer to focus on the few pieces of work that change a company's exposure the most, and to avoid long checklists that satisfy an auditor and change little else. Security is easier to sustain when it is explained in terms the business already uses, such as revenue, deal timing and customer confidence. In New Zealand I also work with mid-market companies that need senior security judgment without hiring a full-time executive. You can read the short version on the Career Highlight page.

Aikido and teaching

I found my Aikido teacher, Richard Moon, in 1987, and I have trained with him ever since. Through the 1990s I taught children and adult classes at Aikido of Mountain View and Aikido of San Francisco. Later I took over Richard's Thursday evening class at City Aikido in San Francisco.

In September 2025, in New Zealand, Richard awarded me a 6th degree black belt in Quantum Aikido. I have chosen not to register it with a federation. The rank reflects the work between a teacher and a student, and I am content to leave it there. Aikido has been the one constant through every job I have held, and it is the reason I treat security as a discipline of attention and response as much as a discipline of controls.

Teaching children taught me to explain an idea without jargon. Teaching adults taught me to wait while someone finds their own answer. Both habits come into every coaching conversation and every security review I run.

Working with Richard Moon

I have been Richard's student for nearly forty years, and for part of that time I have also worked alongside him. Through Quantum Edge I have developed curriculum, edited, and been part of the seminar delivery team for his team building, executive coaching and communications workshops. I edited The Power of Extraordinary Listening, published by Zanshin Press. I also co-created the Moonsensei YouTube channel and the 2014 Create A Beautiful World video magazine.

Richard serves as AikiField's Chief Creative Officer, a creative role. His teaching, writing and views remain his own, and nothing on this site speaks for him. You can read about his work on his own page.

Online communities and tools

In the mid-1990s I ran an early martial arts mailing list, and I helped build and run the websites for City Aikido and Aikido of Mountain View. I enjoy making it easier for people who practice to find one another and learn from one another.

When people ask what I do, I say that I help people see their situation clearly and act on it. The setting changes, whether it is a security program, a board presentation or a difficult conversation with a colleague, but the method does not.

Education

I earned a B.S. in Computer Science at Southeastern Louisiana University, where I studied from 1982 to 1986 and was the founding president of the ACM student chapter.

Where I work

I am based in Wellington, New Zealand, and I work with clients anywhere. I speak English and Spanish.

about this site

a living archive of one teacher's journey

QuantumAikido.com is a website about Richard Moon's body of work: fifty years on the mat, a decade of peace negotiations across three continents, thirty years of executive leadership coaching, including seventeen years teaching in the UTSA Graduate School of Business for Professional Excellence, as it converged into a single understanding and became the book Quantum Aikido: The Power of Harmony. The site documents that journey: the concepts, the field work, the practices, and the teaching lineage that produced them.

Core Concepts

WIGO: What Is Really Going On

Core Concept

WIGO is Richard's primary inquiry framework, developed across fifty years of Aikido practice and refined through executive coaching in the 1990s. Where most people respond to surface appearances (the stated position, the apparent conflict, the visible behavior), WIGO asks what's actually moving beneath. It draws on the quantum observer effect: the quality of your attention shapes what becomes visible. In Cyprus and Bosnia, it was the capacity to sense the fear beneath the anger, the need beneath the demand, the grief beneath the righteous position, that made transformation possible. Not technique. Depth of attention.

The Quantum Pause

Core Concept

The Quantum Pause is the interval between stimulus and response: the space Viktor Frankl identified from inside a concentration camp as the location of human freedom. Richard's decades of Aikido training made this interval physical and repeatable: on the mat, you learn to receive incoming energy without flinching, creating the space to perceive and redirect before acting. Off the mat, the same capacity applies to every charged conversation and triggered reaction. The Quantum Pause doesn't suppress reactivity; it creates conditions for intelligence to respond instead of compulsion. Thousands of repetitions in the dojo. Then, in a room in Cyprus, one Turkish Cypriot stopped, paused, and asked a real question instead of launching a counter-attack. The room changed.

Peace Work

Cyprus: Peace Across the Green Line

Mid-1990s

In the mid-1990s Richard joined a team attempting track-two diplomacy between Greek Cypriot and Turkish Cypriot community leaders, communities divided since the 1974 partition. Working over five years with the Institute for Multi-Track Diplomacy (IMTD), the Harvard Negotiation Project, and the Fulbright Foundation-Cyprus, with support from the American Embassy in Cyprus, and alongside Douglas Stone (who would later write the foreword to Creativity in the Unified Field), the work created genuine dialogue where official channels had failed for thirty years. Cyprus confirmed something the mat had been teaching for decades: that the quality of the facilitators' presence (centered or contracted, open or defended) determines what becomes possible in the room. State is contagious. When the facilitators were present, participants became present. When they were managing process, participants managed positions.

Bosnia: Reconciliation After War

Mid-1990s

The Bosnia work came in the mid-1990s, not long after the Dayton Accords ended a war that killed 100,000 people and displaced more than two million. Working with the Conflict Management Group and the Harvard Negotiation Project, this was different from Cyprus in a fundamental way: not frozen conflict, but fresh grief. The negotiation frameworks designed for interest-based dialogue were not built for the moment when someone across the table lost their child six months ago. Bosnia taught Richard something the mat had been preparing him for: that the capacity to stay present with devastating pain, without flinching, without rushing to resolution, is not a technique but a form of embodied training. Witness is the first act of peace. You cannot reconcile people who have not yet been heard.

Pacific Rim: Harmony Across the Ocean

Nautilus Institute

Through the Nautilus Institute for Security and Sustainability, an Asia-Pacific think tank addressing security, energy, and disarmament, Richard brought Aikido principles to cross-cultural dialogue across the Pacific. What proved unexpectedly powerful was that the framework wasn't alien in these contexts: the Japanese concept of aiki, the Chinese qi, the Korean Won all share the same root understanding of harmony and energy. Rather than imposing Western conflict resolution frameworks, Aikido offered a third space, experiential rather than theoretical, grounded in shared physical vocabulary, in which people from vastly different cultural backgrounds could explore common principles. The Pacific Rim work also revealed a deeper integration: that security and sustainability are not separate domains, and that narrow attention makes you vulnerable to what you're not watching.

Executive Coaching

Corporate Leadership: Thirty Years on the Mat in the Boardroom

1980s–2000s

Beginning in the mid-1980s and continuing through the voicemail coaching era of the 1990s into the 2000s, Moon applied Aikido and Dialogue principles to executive leadership in more than fifty major corporations. His coaching clients have included Apple Computer, Intel, Motorola, Lawrence Livermore Laboratories, Chevron Research, McCaw Cellular, Nextel Communications, and Scandinavian Air Systems. He taught in the University of Texas San Antonio Graduate School of Business for Professional Excellence for seventeen years.

The methodology was developed alongside Chris Thorsen (Quantum Edge) and is documented in the co-authored white papers Leadership of the Spirit and Inspired Leadership (quantumedge.org).

Electronic Coaching: The Voicemail Years

1997 onward

Beginning in 1997, Richard developed an electronic coaching practice that delivered 60-to-120-second teachings via voicemail to corporate clients including Apple, Intel, Motorola, Lawrence Livermore Laboratories, Chevron, McCaw Cellular, Nextel, and Scandinavian Air Systems. Each message explored WIGO, the real energy beneath surface appearances, as immediate, practical wisdom someone could absorb during their commute. He described it as "a swimming pool with a message at the bottom: you have to dive down, feel the pressure, retrieve the wisdom." The form anticipated short-form video by thirty years. That archive of short teachings, thousands of messages organized around the same core principles, is the direct ancestor of Creativity in the Unified Field YouTube channel, and the embodied source material from which the book was eventually distilled.

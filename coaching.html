Coaching

A confidential thinking partner for consequential decisions

Confidential coaching with Kenneth Kron, grounded in Richard Moon's teaching on listening, for leaders facing decisions that matter.

Who the coaching is for

I coach people who carry responsibility for other people: executives, founders, senior practitioners and leadership teams. They are usually capable, and they are usually not stuck for lack of information. What they lack is a place to think out loud with someone who will listen carefully and ask the question that changes the picture.

I am a former CISO and a 6th degree black belt in Quantum Aikido, and I have taught Aikido to children and adults. I also lead with the experience of having sat in the room when a decision could have gone either way. People who work with me tend to be those who value being listened to by someone who has carried responsibility and who still trains.

What brings people here

  • A leader is facing a consequential decision and wants to make it with a clear head.
  • A practitioner is trying to align their work with what they care about most.
  • A founder has found that the organization has outgrown the way it operates.
  • Someone understands the problem well but cannot yet move through it.
  • A leadership team needs a more useful conversation about attention, responsibility, conflict or change.

How this differs from generic advice

Much advice begins with a method. I begin with the situation. We spend the first part of our time on what is happening, who is involved, and what you are noticing in your own body and reactions as you describe it. Then we look for the next move that you can make with conviction. Some sessions end with a decision. Others end with a clearer question, which is often the more useful result at the start. If a conversation is ahead of you, we may rehearse it, so that you can hear how it lands before it counts. I draw on more than twenty years of leading security teams, where the stakes are often high and the information is incomplete, and on nearly forty years of Aikido practice, where you learn to stay steady while something is coming at you.

How Richard Moon's work informs it

The foundation of this coaching is the teaching of Richard Moon, my teacher since 1987. Richard is the creator of Extraordinary Listening and the author of Quantum Aikido: The Power of Harmony. For more than thirty years he has coached executives and teams, and his work on centering, blending and leading is central to how I think about a hard conversation. I have developed curriculum and helped deliver his workshops, and I edited The Power of Extraordinary Listening. Richard serves as AikiField's Chief Creative Officer. He does not deliver this coaching unless we say so, and any joint session happens only when he agrees to it. AikiField does not speak for him.

Coaching teams

Some leaders ask me to work with a team instead of an individual. The same approach applies. We look at how the group listens to itself, where responsibility is unclear, and what is going unsaid. I treat what is said in the room as confidential.

Why Aikido belongs in a leadership conversation

Richard teaches a sequence that I use constantly: center yourself, blend with what is happening, and lead toward something that works. In a hard conversation this means getting steady before you speak, understanding the other person's concern as they hold it, and then proposing a direction that takes it seriously. It sounds simple. It is hard to do under pressure, which is why we practice it.

Confidentiality

What you tell me stays between us. If a situation ever called for sharing something, I would tell you first and ask for your agreement.

What a first conversation involves

A first conversation is confidential. You describe what you are facing, and I ask questions. By the end we both have a better sense of whether coaching is the right kind of help, and whether I am the right person to provide it. There is no obligation to continue. If we do continue, we agree together on how often to meet and what we are working toward, and we revisit that agreement as the situation changes.

What clients may leave with

Clients often leave with a clearer statement of the decision in front of them, a way to have the conversation they have been avoiding, and a steadier way of responding when pressure rises. I do not promise outcomes. The work depends on what you bring to it and what is happening in your organization.

What is out of scope

This is not therapy, and it does not replace medical, legal or financial advice. If I think another kind of help would serve you better, I will say so.

Working with Richard's ideas in your own setting

You do not need to know Aikido to benefit. Nobody is asked to step on a mat, and no physical practice is required. The ideas are applied to the conversation, the decision or the team in front of us, and I explain each one in plain language as we use it. If you are curious about the practice itself, I am glad to point you toward Richard's book and videos.

How to begin

Begin a confidential conversation. Tell me briefly what you are facing and when you would like to talk. I am based in Wellington and meet with clients anywhere. You can read more about me.

### The Story Begins

Egbert had been training for four months now.

His gi — that's the white uniform Aikido students wear — didn't feel strange anymore. He knew how to bow when he entered the dojo. He knew to bow to O Sensei's picture before stepping onto the mat. He knew to say *"Osu"* — a word that means *I understand, I am present, I am ready.*

He was getting good.

He could roll without getting dizzy. He could stay centered when someone pushed him. He could even do the technique called *kokyu-ho* — where you receive someone's energy and redirect it — without using any strength at all.

On the mat, Egbert felt like himself.

The mat-Egbert was calm. The mat-Egbert could breathe. The mat-Egbert knew what to do.

*Off* the mat was another story.

---

> *"The secret of Aikido is to harmonize ourselves with the movement of the universe and bring ourselves into accord with the universe itself."*
>
> — O Sensei

---

It happened on a Tuesday.

Egbert was in math class. His teacher, Mr. Benson, was walking between the desks checking homework. He stopped at Egbert's desk. He looked at Egbert's paper.

He shook his head.

"Egbert," he said — loudly, so everyone could hear — "you've gotten every single one of these wrong. Every. Single. One."

Thirty heads turned to look at him.

Egbert felt something happen inside his chest.

It wasn't quite anger. It wasn't quite fear. It was like both of them arrived at the same time and crashed into each other.

His face went red. His shoulders went up. His breathing went shallow and fast — like a rabbit's.

He wanted to disappear. He wanted to shout. He wanted to cry, which made him want to disappear even more.

He didn't do any of those things.

He just sat there, frozen, staring at his desk.

*Where was mat-Egbert?* he thought. *Why didn't he come?*

---

> *"The people want heaven; they don't know they are heaven."*
>
> — O Sensei

---

That afternoon at the dojo, Sensei Bob was watching.

He always watched. Not in a scary way — in the way that made you feel like someone actually saw you.

After class, he sat down next to Egbert.

"You seem far away today," Sensei said.

Egbert shrugged.

Sensei waited. He was very good at waiting.

"I froze," Egbert finally said. "In school. In front of everyone. I froze like... like I used to. Before Aikido."

Sensei nodded. "Tell me what happened in your body."

Egbert thought about it. "My chest got tight. My breathing went fast. Like a rabbit."

"Yes," Sensei said, as if this was the most interesting thing he'd ever heard. "What happened to your center?"

Egbert didn't have to think long. "It went away."

"It went *up*," Sensei corrected gently. "Your weight went up. Your attention went up — into your thoughts, into your face, into what everyone was thinking about you. You left the ground."

Egbert was quiet.

"The mat," Sensei said simply, "is just practice."

"For what?"

Sensei smiled. "For *everywhere else.*"

---

> *"There are no kata — no set forms — in true Aiki. All phenomena of this world vary constantly according to the particular circumstances and no two situations are exactly alike."*
>
> — O Sensei

---

That night, Egbert lay in bed and tried something.

He imagined he was standing in math class again. He felt the tightness come back into his chest, the redness come back into his face.

Then, the way Sensei had taught him, he let his weight drop.

Not physically — he was lying down — but in his mind, in his *feeling*, he imagined his weight sinking downward. Like a stone settling into the bottom of a still lake.

His breathing slowed.

The tightness in his chest loosened.

*Oh*, he thought. *It works here too.*

He fell asleep still thinking about it.

### The Story Continues

Marcus arrived on a Wednesday.

He was big. Not just tall — *solid*. The kind of big that takes up extra space even when it's standing still.

He walked into the dojo like someone who expected it to be a fight.

His arms were crossed. His jaw was set. His eyes moved quickly around the room, checking everything.

Egbert recognized that look. He had worn it himself once.

*That's someone who's been hurt*, he thought. *And is trying to make sure it doesn't happen again.*

But the thought came and went. Mostly what Egbert felt was his stomach drop, because Sensei Bob paired them together.

---

> *"Martial arts must undergo constant change. It must not stop even for an instant."*
>
> — O Sensei

---

"Partners," Sensei said simply, and pointed.

Egbert bowed to Marcus.

Marcus looked at him like the bow was a trick.

"You have to bow back," Egbert said.

"Why?"

"It means we're going to take care of each other."

Marcus made a sound that wasn't quite a laugh. But he bowed — barely.

They began.

The technique that day was *irimi-nage* — an entering throw. One person grabs, the other moves *in* — not away — and redirects.

Marcus grabbed Egbert's wrist.

He grabbed it *hard*.

Egbert felt his old reflex fire: pull away, get free, defend.

He resisted the reflex. He took a breath. He let his weight drop.

Then he entered — moved toward Marcus, not away — and turned.

Marcus stumbled forward, confused, unbalanced.

He looked down at himself like his own body had betrayed him.

"How did you do that?" he said. He sounded angry. But Egbert noticed his eyes were not angry. His eyes were *interested*.

"You use my energy," Egbert said. "You don't fight it."

Marcus grabbed again. Harder.

Egbert did it again. Weight down. Enter. Turn.

Marcus went down on one knee.

"That's not fair," he said.

Sensei appeared behind them like he'd been there the whole time. "Nothing about the size of your grab changed what happened, Marcus. Notice that."

Marcus stood up. He looked at Egbert differently now.

---

> *"Techniques which are visible to the eye are useless. Your body and the kami — the divine spirit — become one through the accumulation of training."*
>
> — O Sensei

---

But it didn't last.

The next class, Marcus was worse. He grabbed harder. He pushed when it wasn't his turn. When Egbert stumbled, Marcus made a sound like it was funny.

Egbert felt something old and hot rise up in his chest.

*He's doing it on purpose*, Egbert thought. *He's trying to make me look stupid.*

He felt his center go away. He felt his weight go up. He felt the rabbit-breathing come back.

And he pushed back.

Not hard. But he pushed.

And the moment he did, Marcus grinned. Because now they were having a *contest* — and contests were something Marcus understood.

They were both red-faced and out of breath when Sensei's voice came, quiet and clear:

"Stop."

They stopped.

The dojo went very still.

Sensei looked at both of them. Not angry. *Curious.*

"What just happened?" he asked.

Neither of them answered.

"You forgot," Sensei said, "that this is not a fight. A fight happens when both people stop listening." He paused. "What were you listening to, Egbert?"

Egbert thought about it. "My anger," he said. "I was listening to my anger."

"And what was your anger telling you?"

"That he was trying to beat me."

Sensei turned to Marcus. "Was he right?"

A long pause. Then, very quietly, Marcus said: "Yeah."

"Why?"

Marcus shrugged. Then, so quietly almost no one could hear: "It's the only way I know."

---

> *"It is not for correcting others; it is for correcting your own mind. This is Aikido."*
>
> — O Sensei

---

After class, Sue Anne found Egbert sitting outside the dojo.

"You handled that okay," she said.

"I pushed back," he said. "I lost my center."

"You got it back," she said.

He looked at her. "How do you always stay centered?"

Sue Anne thought about it seriously. "I don't," she said. "I just notice faster when I lose it. And I don't make it mean something terrible about myself when I do."

She picked up her bag. "Marcus is scared," she said. "Can't you see it? He covers it with big. But it's there."

She walked away.

Egbert sat there for a while.

He knew about big. He knew about covering things with it. He had tried covering his fear with a punch once. It hadn't worked.

### The Story Continues

It was Egbert who started it.

He didn't plan to. It just happened.

It was lunchtime, two weeks after the Marcus incident. Egbert was crossing the cafeteria with his tray when he saw Marcus.

Marcus was sitting alone.

Not at the edge of a group, pretending not to care. Actually alone — at the far end of a table, back to the wall, watching everyone the way someone watches a place they're not sure is safe.

Egbert stopped walking.

He thought about the day Sue Anne had found him. How she had stepped into the circle of boys. How she hadn't asked permission or announced what she was doing. She had just — moved toward him.

He walked to Marcus's table and sat down.

Marcus looked up. Suspicious.

"What do you want?" Marcus said.

"To sit here," Egbert said.

"Why?"

Egbert picked up his fork. "Because you're sitting alone, and I remember what that felt like."

---

> *"I want considerate people to listen to the voice of Aikido. It is not for correcting others; it is for correcting your own mind."*
>
> — O Sensei

---

For a long time, neither of them said anything.

They ate. The cafeteria was loud around them — trays clattering, kids laughing, someone across the room getting teased about something.

Finally Marcus said: "You used to get picked on."

"Yeah," Egbert said.

"For your name."

"For my name. For being scared. For not knowing what to do with either." He paused. "I punched a kid. Got in trouble. Had to walk home alone. Three boys jumped me." He took a bite. "That's when I met Sue Anne."

Marcus was quiet. Then: "Weren't you embarrassed? That a girl had to save you?"

Egbert smiled a little. "Yeah. For about ten seconds. Then I was just grateful."

"That's not... that's not normal," Marcus said.

"Probably not," Egbert said. "But embarrassment is just another energy. You can fight it, or you can roll with it."

Marcus looked at him. "That's from Aikido."

"Yeah."

"Everything with you is from Aikido."

"Pretty much," Egbert said.

And Marcus — for the first time Egbert had seen — almost smiled.

---

> *"There is no enemy of love. There is no discord in love. Aikido is the realization of love."*
>
> — O Sensei

---

The next week, something had shifted between them.

Not dramatically. Not like a movie. Just — shifted.

In class, when Marcus grabbed Egbert's wrist, he grabbed it honestly instead of hard. There is a difference. Hard is about winning. Honest is about training.

Egbert felt it.

He entered, turned, redirected.

Marcus went with it this time. Smooth and clean, like water.

They both stopped and looked at each other.

"That was different," Marcus said.

"You didn't resist," Egbert said.

"Neither did you."

"That's called Aiki," Egbert said. "When two people stop fighting the energy and just... move together." He tried to remember Sensei's words. "One plus one equals one."

Marcus made a face. "That's not math."

"No," Egbert agreed. "It's better."

---

> *"The secret of Aikido is to harmonize with the movement of the universe and bring ourselves into accord with the universe itself."*
>
> — O Sensei

---

On a Thursday after class, Sensei Bob asked Marcus to stay behind.

Egbert waited outside. He wasn't sure why.

When Marcus came out, his face was different. Not broken. Not fixed. Just — *open*, the way a window looks when someone has pushed it up and let in air.

"He knew," Marcus said. "About home."

Egbert didn't ask how. Sensei always knew.

"Is it bad?" Egbert said.

"It's... loud," Marcus said. And that was all. But Egbert understood that loud could mean many things that weren't about sound.

"You can train with me," Egbert said. "After school, before class. I'll show you the centering exercise. The one for when things get loud."

Marcus looked at him. "Why?"

Egbert thought about it. "Because Sue Anne didn't have to stop for me," he said. "But she did. And it changed everything."

He picked up his bag.

"And because," he said, "Sensei Bob says the real dojo is everywhere. That means the mat goes wherever you go. Even into loud."

---

> *"Martial arts must develop in an evolutionary manner together with the movements of the heavenly bodies and must not stop even for an instant."*
>
> — O Sensei

---

The following Saturday was a testing day.

Egbert had passed his test months ago. Now he watched as two of the newer students were tested — including Marcus, who had moved faster than anyone expected.

Marcus's technique was rough. But his center held.

At the end, Sensei bowed to each student. Then the students bowed to each other.

Egbert watched Marcus bow. Watched him really *bow* — not the barely-a-nod from his first day. A real bow. The kind that meant: *I see you. I will take care of you. We are in this together.*

Marcus straightened up and caught Egbert's eye across the dojo.

He nodded.

Egbert nodded back.

Some things don't need words. Some things live in the space between two people when both of them have decided to stop fighting and start listening.

That space, Sensei Bob once said, is where Aikido lives.

That space is Aiki-land.

## A Glossary for Curious Readers

**Aikido** *(eye-KEE-doe)* — A Japanese martial art based on harmony. The name means "the way of harmony with the energy of the universe."

**Dojo** *(DOH-joe)* — The place where Aikido is practiced. It means "place of the way." But as Sensei Bob says, the real dojo is everywhere.

**Gi** *(GEE)* — The white uniform worn during training.

**Irimi-nage** *(ee-REE-mee NAH-gay)* — An entering throw. Instead of moving away from an attack, you move *in*, then redirect.

**Ki** *(KEY)* — Life energy. The force that flows through all living things. When it flows freely, we feel alive, clear, and strong. When it gets blocked by fear or anger or tension, we feel stuck.

**Kokyu-ho** *(KO-kyoo-HO)* — A breathing exercise that teaches how to receive and redirect energy without using strength.

**O Sensei** *(OH-SEN-say)* — "Great Teacher." The title given to Morihei Ueshiba, the founder of Aikido. He lived from 1883 to 1969.

**Osu** *(OSS)* — A word used in Aikido that carries many meanings at once: *I understand. I am here. I am ready. I will try.*

**Randori** *(RAN-doh-ree)* — Free practice with multiple partners at the same time. It teaches you to stay centered when everything is moving at once.

**Sensei** *(SEN-say)* — Teacher. It means "one who has walked the path before."

**Ukemi** *(OO-keh-mee)* — The art of falling and rolling safely. In Aikido, learning to fall is just as important as learning to throw — because when you know how to fall, you are no longer afraid of it.

**Zanshin** *(ZAN-shin)* — Continuing awareness. The feeling of presence that doesn't stop when the technique is over. Zanshin means staying awake to everything, all the time.

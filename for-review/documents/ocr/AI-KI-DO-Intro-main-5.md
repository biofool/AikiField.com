***PRINCIPLES OF AIKIDO***

AS TAUGHT BY THE FOUNDER

* *The secret of Aikido is to harmonize ourselves with the movement of the universe and bring ourselves into accord with the universe itself.*

* *Aiki is not a technique to fight with or defeat the enemy. It is the way to reconcile the world an d make human beings one family.*

* *Winning means winning over the mind of discord in yourself. It is to accomplish your bestowed mission.*

* *Aikido is non-resistance. As it is non-resistance, it is always victorious.*

* *My students think I don’t loose my center: that is not so. I simply recognize it sooner and return more quickly.*

* *A mind to serve for the peace of all human beings in the world is needed in Aikido, and not the mind of one who wishes to be strong or who practices only to fell an opponent.*

* *Aikido is the realization of love.*

***An Aikido Guided Life***

Aikido has guided my life for fifty years. I began practicing the art to help me manage a high stress role as a crisis counselor for which I had no formal training. Aikido principles and practices became central to that work as well as many subsequent strategic consulting engagements developing harmonious cultures and intuitive leaders in the corporate world that led to industry breakthroughs. For several years we shared Aikido’s fun, slow-motion, non-falling exercises with Greek and Turkish policy leaders to engage them in an embodied dialogue for peace on the war torn island of Cyprus. [See [Quantum Edge](https://quantumedge.org/) for stories]

I had the good fortune to find Richard Moon as my Aikido teacher and his teacher, Robert Nadeau, who trained directly with O’Sensei, the founder of the art. They emphasized directly sensing energy for intuitive spontaneity beyond the set techniques of traditional Japanese training. Richard became my consulting partner in Quantum Edge where we put this understanding into practice in our leadership development work.

With sincere engagement in this approach, we have consistently seen leaders deepen their energy awareness and demonstrate generative presence under pressure. With such deep presence comes more sensitive situational awareness, stronger relational harmony for decisive collaboration in complex systems and the ability to lead intuitively using change itself as a source of power. Yet beyond the increase in personal power and influence, the real goal of our inquiry into Aikido has been the awakening of unitive awareness and the realization of one’s true nature – open consciousness.

O’Sensei said that he learned the art from nature. My roots too are in nature, the fields lakes and woods of farm country in Illinois, later the Sierra Nevada and Wind River mountains. My parents shared a deep love of nature and supported my wandering solitude in the outdoors.

I discovered Haiku poetry in my early twenties and then met my friend and mentor, the premier haiku poet in English, JW Hackett, who took me under his wing and taught me The Way of Haiku as a way of living. The result is the lifetime of poems you’ll find in Wandering in Wonder. Critically, Jim taught me Za Zen, awakening my life long inquiry into consciousness.

Fifty-five years of za zen,

fifty of Aikido. . .

now, just this breath.

- CT

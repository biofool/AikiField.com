About Richard Moon

Richard Moon, teacher of Aikido's inner art

Richard Moon is a 6th Dan Aikido teacher, mediator, executive coach, musician and author of Quantum Aikido: The Power of Harmony.

Who he is

Richard Moon is one of the world's foremost teachers of Aikido's inner art. He is a 6th Dan sensei, and he is also a mediator, executive coach and musician. He has taught Aikido around the world for more than fifty years. In the mid-1990s he was on the staff of a weeklong mediation project in Cyprus, where Douglas Stone, a Harvard Law lecturer and co-author of Difficult Conversations, first met him.

Teaching

Richard teaches seminars internationally, and in September 2025 his teaching took him to New Zealand. His approach treats Aikido as a way of life more than a way of fighting. He is the creator of Extraordinary Listening, a body of teaching on how attention, presence and listening change what is possible between people.

In simple terms, the practice teaches you to meet force without meeting it with force, and to stay present when a situation begins to move quickly. On the mat that means training with a partner. In a meeting, a family or a negotiation, it means noticing what is happening before deciding how to respond. Richard calls this the inner art of Aikido, and it is the thread that connects his teaching in the dojo with his work in organizations.

Peace and mediation work

The Cyprus project is where Douglas Stone met Richard. Stone lectured at Harvard Law School for 35 years and wrote the foreword to Richard's book. He is a useful witness to Richard's work because he saw it in a setting where the stakes for the participants were real.

Mediation asks people to stay centered, to listen to someone they disagree with, and to keep a conversation open when it would be easier to close it. That is the same practice Richard teaches in the dojo and in organizations.

Executive coaching

For more than thirty years Richard has coached executives and teams across telecommunications, construction, utilities and international project management. With Christopher Thorsen he wrote the white paper Leadership of the Spirit, which brings together Aikido's way of centering, blending and leading with the practice of dialogue.

In practice, the work asks leaders to center themselves before a difficult conversation, to blend with the other person's concern instead of pushing against it, and to lead the conversation toward something both sides can use. Organizations come to Richard when a decision is stuck, when a team is divided, or when leaders sense that the conversation in the room is not the one that needs to happen.

Writing

Richard's book Quantum Aikido: The Power of Harmony was published by Park Street Press in January 2026, with a foreword by Douglas Stone. He is also a co-author of Aikido: The Art of Transformation. His Extraordinary Listening titles, which include The Power of Extraordinary Listening and The Business of Listening, are available free at ExtraordinaryListening.com.

The book describes how the principles of harmony he has taught for decades work in practice. It is written for people who train in Aikido and for readers who have never stepped on a mat. Richard's own order of events matters here: the practice came first, and the language of quantum physics came later, because it matched what he was already doing in the dojo and with the people he coached.

Music

In his own words, music came first for Richard, before yoga and before Aikido. Douglas Stone describes him as a musician who plays in many styles and lives as an improviser. You can read more on the page about Richard's Aikido and music.

Food

Moon also describes himself as a food designer. He has worked in kitchens that include the Aware Inn, The Source on the Sunset Strip, and Wild Mountain Cafe.

His role at AikiField

Richard serves as AikiField's Chief Creative Officer, a creative role in which he helps shape how the practice is presented. Kenneth Kron, who leads AikiField, has been Richard's student since 1987, and the two have worked together on workshops, writing and video. AikiField does not own, represent or speak for Richard. His teaching, his writing and his opinions are his own, and he decides what is said in his name.

A note on his approach

Across all of these settings Richard teaches the same thing in different words. Slow down enough to notice what is really happening, stay connected to the person in front of you, and act from that clarity instead of from habit or fear.

Where to begin

If you are new to Richard's work, the book is the best place to start, because it sets out the ideas in order. The Moonsensei videos and the Ki to the City podcast are the quickest way to hear how he teaches. The free Extraordinary Listening titles are suited to readers who want something practical to take into the next meeting.

Where to find his work

Richard signs off his teaching with a line that sums up the work: Don't get mad. Don't even get even. Create a beautiful world.

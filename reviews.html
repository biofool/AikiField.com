foreword writer

"I remember clear as yesterday the first time I met Richard. It was the mid-1990s, and I was teaching conflict resolution at Harvard Law School. We were both part of the staff working on a weeklong mediation project in Cyprus. When he walked in around midnight, he was smiling and calm, and had a mischievous gleam in his eye. Sensing the room's energy, he made a remark along the lines of, 'If you look to the totality, what's wrong points to what's right. Envision what tomorrow would be if it were great.' Then with a laugh, he added, 'Of course, I could be wrong. Anything can happen.'

My spirits lifted. Our new arrival was not just confident, he was also funny. He knew how to change the mood in a room with a single observation or question. Whatever surprises were in store for us over the next week, Richard, through the harmonious power of Aikido, would know what to say and do.

"Richard is not a wizard. He's a person like you and me. His power to move people beyond themselves is not based on some supernatural enlightenment but resides in his very humanness—his studious intelligence, his compassion, his insight. He sees the world differently from most people, and yet he is able to see each of us just as we want to be seen."

Douglas Stone

Lecturer, Harvard Law School (35 years)
Co-author, Difficult Conversations: How to Discuss What Matters Most

Reader Reviews

Quantum Aikido: The Power of Harmony — Richard Moon

Creativity in the Unified Field is one of the most conceptually rigorous examinations of Aikido’s internal principles currently available. Richard Moon articulates the art through the lenses of somatics, relational dynamics, and quantum theory, offering a framework that aligns directly with the methodological foundations of our dojo.

Moon’s work is not concerned with technical accumulation. Instead, it investigates the pre‑technical conditions that make technique viable: embodied awareness, coherent intention, and the capacity to perceive relational fields before they crystallize into conflict.

Harmony as Physiological Organization

Moon’s assertion that harmony is a measurable physiological state — rather than a philosophical abstraction — is central to our training. When the body is organized around openness and coherence, conflict cannot attach.

Musubi as Field Dynamics

His interpretation of musubi as a shared quantum field provides a sophisticated model for understanding relational emergence. This aligns with our emphasis on connection as the primary determinant of technique.

Embodied Awareness as Technical Foundation

Moon identifies softening, attentional expansion, and centered responsiveness as the core competencies from which effective technique emerges. This reflects our dojo’s state‑based pedagogy.

Conflict as Energetic Integration

By reframing conflict as energy requiring integration rather than resistance, Moon situates Aikido within a broader systems‑theory context. This perspective is foundational to our approach.

Aikido as Consciousness Discipline

Moon positions Aikido as a study of perception and relational cognition. This is consistent with our view of Aikido as a consciousness practice rather than a purely martial discipline.

Creativity in the Unified Field is not merely recommended reading. It is an essential conceptual companion to our training — a text that articulates the principles we explore experientially on the mat.

Kourosh  💐🙏
